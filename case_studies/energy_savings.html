£2.3M in Energy Savings via Weekly Intervention Calls

How a weekly intervention rhythm across 1,000+ London pumping stations produced £2.3M in energy savings at Thames Water.

The problem

Over 1,000 waste pumping stations across London North, London South, and Mogden — each one drawing power, each one occasionally under-performing or failing. The estate had multiple monitoring signals, but no single evidence-based way to prioritise interventions each week. Reactive maintenance dominated the queue, and energy consumption stayed higher than it needed to be.

My role

I own and lead the Weekly Intervention Call — the rhythm that decides which sites get attention, in what order, based on what evidence. I am accountable for the tracker, the escalations, and the outcome measurement.

The approach

Consolidate everything into a single weekly view, then run a tight rhythm on top of it.

  • Unified signal set. Pump Run-Time, SCADA alarm trends, Stressed Site Reporting, Energy Tracker, and Pump Behaviour — all rolled into one weekly tracker so we prioritise against the same picture.
  • Backlog first, then new. Each call starts by walking the existing backlog — has the site’s behaviour changed, has work been done, do we reprioritise? Only then do we add new stressed sites picked up by the signals.
  • Evidence in front of regional managers. Newly added sites are presented to the respective regional managers with the underlying data. Debates about priority get settled with the numbers, not memory.
  • Close the loop on savings. For every site where an intervention completes, we check the energy profile against baseline — so savings are measured, not assumed.
  • Planned vs. reactive review. Each call also reviews planned and reactive jobs on the estate, keeping the pipeline honest.

The result

  • £2.3M in energy savings across 2025–2026 from optimisations driven by the intervention pipeline.
  • Acknowledged by the CEO in an all-hands.
  • Established a repeatable weekly cadence and a shared source of truth for site prioritisation across three regions.

What it unlocked

The savings figure is the easy headline. The harder-to-quantify win is that regional managers now have a defensible list of interventions, backed by evidence, that they can act on and report upwards. Reactive work still happens — but it happens on top of a prioritised plan, not instead of one.

What I would tell someone doing this again

The trick was consolidating half a dozen different reports into one weekly view. Once the weekly rhythm was set and everyone was looking at the same tracker, most of the priority debates dissolved and the work started shipping.
