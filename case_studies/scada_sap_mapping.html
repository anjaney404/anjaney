SCADA/SAP Alarm-to-Asset Mapping

How I helped lift Loddon asset availability compliance from 10% to 85% in a year at Thames Water.

The problem

At Thames Water, SCADA holds the alarms and SAP holds the asset register — but the two systems do not talk to each other. That leaves a gap: when an alarm fires, it is not immediately obvious which asset it belongs to. Multiplied across hundreds of sites, that gap silently corrupts every metric that depends on the alarm-to-asset relationship, including asset availability compliance.

In the Loddon region, the practical result was a compliance figure of ~10% — not because the estate was performing badly, but because nobody trusted the number.

My role

I acted as the technical lead and internal consultant for the SCADA / SAP mapping workshops. My job was to design the working process, run the sessions with technical managers and the SCADA team, and make sure the outputs were structured well enough to flow back into the daily availability calculation — not just live in someone’s laptop.

The approach

Scope small, ship fast, iterate. We treated Loddon as a proof of concept:

  • Workshop-based reconciliation. For each site, we walked through the active alarm codes and traced them back to the physical asset in SAP, with a subject-matter expert in the room. No guessing.
  • Controlled mapping table. Captured mappings in a shared, version-controlled table with clear ownership and a review process — not a personal spreadsheet.
  • Feed back into the availability calc. The reconciled table plugs directly into the daily availability computation, so compliance starts reflecting reality rather than a broken join.
  • Design for rollout, not just Loddon. The workshop template, the mapping schema, and the review cadence were all built to be copy-pasted into other regions with minimal change.

The result

  • 94% of alarms mapped to their assets in the Loddon region within the proof-of-concept window.
  • Performance compliance climbed from 10% to 85% in a single year — because the metric was finally trustworthy.
  • Approved for rollout to other regions across the estate, and being adapted for the waste pumping station network.

What it unlocked

The headline number is compliance. The bigger win is that teams stopped arguing about whether the metric was real, and started acting on it. It also unblocked downstream work — you cannot build a credible early-warning model on top of an untrusted asset map, and now we can.

What I would tell someone doing this again

The technical work was almost the smallest part. The larger challenges were (1) getting time on the calendars of the right SMEs and (2) making sure the mapping lived somewhere maintainable rather than in a personal spreadsheet. Design for maintenance from day one.
